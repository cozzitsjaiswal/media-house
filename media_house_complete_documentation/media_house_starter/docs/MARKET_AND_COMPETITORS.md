# Market & Competitor Research — Media House

**Research date:** 13 October 2026  
**Geography:** Maharashtra first; wider India later  
**Research standard:** official vendor pages and primary documentation where possible. Product feature pages are vendor claims; they establish competitor positioning, not independent efficacy or Media House demand.

## Executive finding

The category is competitive and mature. Businesses can already buy broad business suites, CRM/marketing automation, local billing tools, restaurant POS, and clinic-management software. Media House should not start by building all those categories from scratch. The more defensible entry is a **verticalized implementation and automation service** that solves one measurable workflow, connects with existing software where practical, and adds a supported operational layer. Repeated implementation patterns can later become reusable software.

### What current sources show

- Odoo positions itself as an integrated suite spanning CRM, eCommerce, accounting, inventory, POS and project tools (official Odoo Apps page: https://apps.odoo.com/apps).
- Petpooja describes restaurant billing/KOT, inventory deduction and reports, online order handling, CRM, and a sizeable integration/add-on ecosystem (https://www.petpooja.com/poss). That breadth makes an all-new restaurant POS an expensive place to start.
- Practo Ray advertises clinic calendar, billing, patient charting, appointment confirmation/reminder/follow-up SMS and other healthcare workflows (https://www.practo.com/providers/clinics/ray/plans). Any adjacent clinic automation still requires strict data and safety controls.
- HighLevel’s official support pricing guide explains subscription tiers plus usage-based features/AI/workflow charges (https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000001156-highlevel-pricing-guide). Media House must model usage costs rather than selling “unlimited automation” without limits.
- Sarvam’s official docs describe speech-to-text, text-to-speech, translation, code-mixed inputs and voice-agent capabilities across Indian languages (https://docs.sarvam.ai/). This makes it a potential candidate for Hindi/Marathi/English prototypes, but voice quality, telephony, latency, per-minute cost, data handling and Marathi accuracy must be tested in a pilot.

## Candidate market wedge

### Primary hypothesis: lead-to-revenue workflow for small B2B service companies

Target companies where enquiries arrive via website, email, referrals, phone or messaging, but follow-up and quote tracking are inconsistent. Examples for research—not yet approved target lists—include maintenance/service contractors, small agencies, consultants, local B2B providers and distributors.

The outcome to sell: “Every enquiry gets recorded, assigned, followed up, and tracked to a quote/win/loss, with reporting on missed follow-ups.” This is narrower and easier to measure than “complete software for every business.”

### Secondary hypothesis: inbound appointment automation

A clinic or professional office receives many appointment calls and staff cannot answer consistently. An AI receptionist can capture basic scheduling details and check confirmed availability. This could be valuable, but healthcare-related data, consent, errors, and safety raise the risk. Pilot first with a non-clinical appointment workflow or with an appropriately reviewed clinic partner; never let a voice agent diagnose or make unsupported care recommendations.

### Later hypothesis: restaurant operations integrations

Restaurants already use POS and ordering products. Explore integration, reporting, follow-up, low-stock notices or internal workflow automation around current systems before offering to replace the POS. The first customer problem must be evidenced in interviews.

## What competitors leave open (hypotheses, not established market gaps)

1. A very specific workflow sold as a fixed-scope implementation rather than a broad feature list.
2. Local-language onboarding/support in Hindi and Marathi with English documentation where useful.
3. A written data-flow and deployment choice: client-hosted, client-local, managed tenant-isolated, or hybrid.
4. AI-generated demo and proposal grounded in the prospect’s public business context, reviewed by a human.
5. Client discovery translated into a requirements sheet with measurable acceptance tests.
6. Integration-first delivery and clear offboarding/data export.
7. Monthly reports showing workflow health and time saved only where actually measured.
8. Reusable delivery playbooks for a narrow niche.

Validate every claimed gap through interviews, competitor demos/reviews and paid pilots; do not market features as unique without checking.

## Validation questions for the first 15–20 interviews

- How do you currently capture and follow up on enquiries?
- What is missed, duplicated or delayed most often?
- What software do you already pay for?
- What happens when a staff member is unavailable?
- What is the cost of the problem in time, lost appointments, missed quotes or errors?
- What tools must the new workflow integrate with?
- What data may not leave the business environment?
- Who decides and who pays?
- What evidence would make you trust a prototype?
- Would you pay for a fixed-scope pilot? What price and terms would make sense?

Do not lead the respondent to agree. Record the current workflow, frequency, consequences, current workaround, willingness-to-pay evidence and objection verbatim where permission allows.

## Competitive strategy decision

Start as an **AI-assisted workflow implementation service**, not as an ERP replacement vendor. Select one workflow and one buyer profile. Deliver with proven tools and limited custom code. If 3–5 clients repeatedly need essentially the same module, evaluate whether to productize it into SaaS. These counts are operational hypotheses, not industry benchmarks.

## Market-source limitations

- Vendor websites reflect vendor claims and may omit limitations.
- Feature availability and pricing change; reconfirm in a live vendor demo/quotation.
- This is not a total addressable market (TAM) estimate. Do not invent TAM, conversion rates, margins or demand from software popularity.
- Local Maharashtra competitor and customer discovery remains an outstanding task.
