# Business Model and Unit Economics

## 1. Recommended model

Begin as a productized automation implementation service; gradually turn repeated parts into a managed platform/subscription. This can pursue revenue before a broad SaaS is built, while creating evidence for what should become a product.

Revenue streams:
1. Paid workflow audit/discovery.
2. Fixed-scope implementation/setup.
3. Monthly maintenance, hosting and support (scope explicit).
4. Usage-based voice, AI, SMS/WhatsApp or payment-provider pass-throughs (transparent and capped).
5. Custom integrations/change requests.
6. Later: subscription for reusable modules.

## 2. First offer hypothesis

**Lead-to-Revenue Workflow for small service businesses**

Outcome: new enquiries are recorded, assigned, followed up, moved through quote/won/lost stages and reported on.

Possible included functions:
- intake form/import;
- prospect or customer record;
- follow-up task and reminders;
- meeting status;
- quotation/proposal tracking;
- basic pipeline dashboard;
- setup, documentation and training.

Excluded unless priced separately: bulk outreach, guaranteed leads, telephony setup, unlimited integrations, bespoke ERP, regulated clinical records, custom mobile app, 24/7 SLA.

## 3. Price hypotheses

Do not call these market rates. Use them as interview/test candidates and adjust after vendor quotations, delivery time and willingness-to-pay evidence.

- Paid workflow audit: ₹0–₹5,000 for the first validation design; free only if time cost is deliberately budgeted.
- Small fixed-scope implementation: ₹15,000–₹40,000 setup hypothesis.
- Ongoing support/workflow monitoring: ₹3,000–₹10,000/month hypothesis.
- More complex integration or voice automation: individually scoped; do not quote before calculating provider charges and compliance effort.

With ₹0 starting capital, prefer paid discovery, milestone payments, client-owned accounts and client-funded provider costs. Never sign up for an expensive dependency or promise delivery that cannot be financed.

## 4. Unit-economics worksheet

For each offer, calculate per client/project:

`Contribution = setup revenue + subscription revenue during period - delivery labour cost - hosting - AI/voice/messaging usage - payment fees - subcontractors - support labour - refunds/credits - other variable costs`.

Measure cash flow separately:
- cash collected before work begins;
- vendor costs due before client payment;
- payment settlement delay;
- tax and refund liabilities;
- ongoing support commitment after initial invoice.

Avoid unlimited “AI included” contracts. Include fair-usage limits and a change-request policy. Price voice/AI according to tested cost per successful workflow, not only per API request.

## 5. Example only—not a forecast

Suppose a project has ₹25,000 setup revenue. Direct delivery labour is costed at ₹8,000; software/provider costs ₹2,000; payment/admin costs ₹500; support credit/contingency ₹1,500. The illustrative contribution is ₹13,000 before owner overhead, taxes, sales acquisition and fixed costs. If the founder does not cost their own hours, this margin will look artificially high.

Suppose the recurring fee is ₹5,000/month, with ₹1,200/month average hosting/provider usage and ₹1,500/month support labour. Contribution before fixed overhead and tax is ₹2,300/month. These are hypothetical values; replace each with actuals after a pilot.

## 6. How to approach 100 clients

100 clients × ₹5,000/month = ₹5,00,000 monthly recurring revenue only if all 100 are active and paying the same price. It is not profit. Subtract staff/support labour, infrastructure, vendor/API usage, payment fees, taxes, churn replacement and acquisition cost.

Model:
- active clients by month;
- new clients, churn and collection rates;
- average setup revenue and delivery hours;
- recurring fee and variable support cost/client;
- CAC and sales time;
- cash collection delay;
- gross/contribution margin;
- support capacity and incidents.

Do not build hiring and infrastructure around 100 customers until early cohorts show retention and manageable support burden.

## 7. Pricing gates

Before issuing a proposal, define:
- fixed deliverables and explicit exclusions;
- acceptance criteria;
- client dependencies/access responsibilities;
- data/hosting model;
- milestone payments;
- support hours and response expectation;
- third-party account ownership and usage charges;
- scope change price/process;
- cancellation/refund/termination and data export/delete terms.
